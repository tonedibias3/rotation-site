How to organize your closet

You can sort a closet by color, type or season. This guide adds one more step that makes the biggest difference: sort by how often you actually wear things.

8 stepsAny closet sizeStart with what you wear

Start with a plan

Set aside a couple of hours on a day when you are not rushed. Grab four bags or boxes and label them Keep, Maybe, Donate and Fix.

1Take everything out

Empty the whole closet onto your bed or the floor. Shelves and drawers too. It looks like a mess for a while. That is the point. You cannot judge what you own while it is packed in the back of a shelf.

Wipe down the shelves and rods while they are empty.

2Sort by type

Make piles for tops, bottoms, outerwear, dresses, shoes, bags and accessories. Now you can see how much you own in each group, including the things you forgot about.

3Check how often you wear each piece

Go through each pile and ask when you last wore the piece. If you cannot remember, that tells you something.

Memory is a rough guide. If you log your outfits, you can use real wear counts instead. In Rotation, the closet view shows what you wear most and which pieces have been resting. It also works out cost per wear once you add a price.

4Decide: keep, maybe or donate

  • Keep what you wear and like wearing.
  • Donate what does not fit and what you keep passing over.
  • Put the maybe pieces in a box and write the date on it. If you have not opened the box in three months, let them go.

A common rule of thumb: if you have not worn something in a year and it is not for a special occasion, it is a candidate to donate.

5Fix and clean what is left

Take the Fix bag to a tailor or repair what you can yourself. Wash or dry clean anything that is going back in dirty. Hanging dirty clothes back up just moves the problem.

6Set up zones

Put what you wear every week at eye level, where you can reach it. Special occasion and seasonal pieces go higher or lower. Off-season clothes can go in bins or on a second rod.

Group by type first. If you like to see it, sort by color inside each group.

7Hang or fold

Hang shirts, jackets, dresses and trousers you want to keep free of creases. Fold knits and sweaters, since hanging can stretch them out. Use drawers for tees, underwear and workout gear.

8Give shoes, bags, accessories and scents a home

Shoes do better with a little space between pairs, on a rack or a shelf. Lightly stuff bags so they hold their shape. Use a tray or a small box for belts, hats and jewelry. If you collect fragrances, keep them out of direct sun and heat.

If you want to track which pairs you actually rotate through, see the shoe rotation guide.

Keep it organized

Use a one in, one out rule. When you buy something new, move one piece out. Check the closet at the start of each season. A quick ten minute check beats another full empty out.

Let an app do the counting

A closet organizer app keeps the wear counts for you, so your next clean out is based on what you wore and not what you remember. Rotation is free during the beta, has no account, and keeps your data on your device.

Questions

How do I organize a small closet?

Use the same steps, but be stricter in step 4. Keep what you wear. Store off-season pieces in bins under the bed or on a high shelf. Slim hangers help, and the floor under hanging clothes can hold shoes or a small rack.

Should I organize my closet by color or by type?

Start with type, so all tops are together and all pants are together. Then sort by color inside each group if you like. Type first makes it faster to find what you want.

How often should I clean out my closet?

A quick check at the start of each season works well. Do the full process, with everything out, about once a year.

How do I decide what to get rid of?

Look at how often you wear it, whether it fits, and whether you like wearing it. A common rule of thumb is to donate anything you have not worn in a year, unless it is for a special occasion. Wear counts make this easier. See the cost per wear guide.
