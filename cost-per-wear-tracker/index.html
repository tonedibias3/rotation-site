Cost per wear, explained

Cost per wear is what you paid for something divided by how many times you have worn it. It shows what a piece really costs you, not what the tag said.

No accountFree in betaStays on your device

1The formula

Price divided by number of wears. That is all it is.

$120÷4 wears=$30 per wear
$120÷40 wears=$3 per wear
Example

A $120 jacket worn 4 times costs $30 per wear.

Wear the same jacket 40 times and it costs $3 per wear.

Example numbers only. They are not data from the app.

2Why it is worth tracking

  • A cheap piece you never wear can cost more per wear than an expensive one you wear every week.
  • It shows which pieces are earning their spot and which are just sitting in the closet.
  • It gives you a better question before you buy: how many times will I really wear this?

3What counts as a good number

There is no magic number. Compare similar pieces. A winter coat worn dozens of times a season will land very differently than a dress shirt worn twice. What matters is the direction. The number only goes down when you wear the piece.

4Tracking it by hand

You need two things for every piece: the price and a running count of wears. A spreadsheet works. The hard part is keeping the count up to date every day.

5How Rotation does it

Add a price when you add a piece. Log your outfit each day and Rotation counts the wears and works out cost per wear for you. It also shows what you wear most and which pieces have been resting.

Prices and where you bought something are never shown on a share card, so you can post a fit without posting what it cost.
